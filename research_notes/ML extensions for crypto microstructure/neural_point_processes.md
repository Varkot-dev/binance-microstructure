# Neural temporal point processes versus parametric Hawkes kernels for trade arrival times (CPU-only)

Research date: 2026-11-17. Target: Binance USDT-M perp aggTrades, ms (later µs) timestamps, one event per aggressor order, business time. Existing fits: exponential MLE (median n = 0.71), two-exponential (0.85), count-variance estimator (0.96).

## 1. Model families and their mechanics

### Takeaway
All neural TPPs are "history encoder (RNN or attention) + conditional distribution head for the next inter-event time"; they differ mainly in whether the head needs numerical integration (Neural Hawkes, THP, SAHP: Monte Carlo compensator), has a closed-form compensator but rigid shape (RMTPP), or has a closed-form flexible density (LogNormMix, spline flows). For a unimarked trade-arrival sequence, the head matters more than the encoder, and the closed-form mixture head is the only family that is flexible, exactly normalised, cheap, and gives closed-form CDF (needed for time-rescaling residuals and interval-censored likelihood).

### Cited Findings

**RMTPP (Du, Dai, Trivedi, Upadhyay, Gomez-Rodriguez, Song; KDD 2016)**
- [R] RNN hidden state h_j summarises history; conditional intensity is log-linear in elapsed time: lambda*(t) = exp(v·h_j + w (t − t_j) + b). The compensator is closed-form and the implied inter-event density is Gompertz-like, i.e. unimodal/monotone hazard between events, a strong shape restriction. — [Du et al. 2016, KDD](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2016/papers/files/rpp1081-duA.pdf)
- [V] In Shchur et al. 2020, RMTPP is one of the "less flexible baselines" that LogNormMix beats on NLL on almost all datasets. — [Shchur, Bilos, Gunnemann 2020, ICLR, arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12127)

**Neural Hawkes Process, NHP (Mei and Eisner; NeurIPS 2017, arXiv 1612.09328)**
- [R] Continuous-time LSTM: memory cells jump at each event and decay exponentially toward a target between events; intensity lambda_k(t) = scaled-softplus(w_k · h(t)), so it can be non-monotone between events and can express inhibition. Log-likelihood integral has no closed form: estimated by Monte Carlo sampling of time points; sampling uses thinning. — [Mei and Eisner 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09328)
- [V] Shchur et al. 2020 frame the intensity-based approach as incurring trade-offs that the intensity-free (direct density) approach avoids. — [arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12127)

**FullyNN (Omi, Ueda, Aihara; NeurIPS 2019, arXiv 1905.09690)**
- [R] Models the cumulative hazard Phi(tau | h) with a monotone feed-forward network (non-negative weights, monotone activations); intensity obtained by automatic differentiation dPhi/dtau; exact likelihood with no numerical integration. — [Omi et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09690)
- [V] Known defect: Shchur et al. 2020 state the FullyNN PDF does not integrate to 1 "due to a suboptimal choice of the network architecture", and the model can assign non-zero probability mass to (−inf, 0); the appendix argument is that a proper density needs the cumulative intensity to diverge as tau → inf, but FullyNN's stays bounded. — [arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.12127)
- [V] Expectation cannot be computed in closed form for FullyNN and sampling needs iterative root finding. — [arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.12127)
- [V] EasyTPP (ICLR 2024) repeats that FullyNN's PDF does not integrate to 1; a 2024 cumulative-hazard paper says FullyNN "fails to define a valid CHF". — [EasyTPP paper PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/e66233a208ef32f56df6312263239fa0-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv 2404.13663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13663)
- [V] FullyNN is not applicable to event-type prediction in EasyTPP (irrelevant for unimarked trade arrivals, relevant if buy/sell marks are added). — [EasyTPP summary](https://liner.com/review/easytpp-towards-open-benchmarking-temporal-point-processes)

**Intensity-free / LogNormMix (Shchur, Bilos, Gunnemann; ICLR 2020 spotlight, arXiv 1909.12127)**
- [V] Directly models the conditional distribution of inter-event times instead of the intensity, drawing on normalizing flows; the log-normal mixture matches flow flexibility while allowing sampling and moments in closed form. — [arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12127); [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2020/poster/1564)
- [R] Mechanics: p(tau | c_i) = sum_k w_k LogNormal(tau; mu_k, s_k), where (w, mu, s) are affine functions of a context vector c_i (RNN history embedding, optionally metadata and a per-sequence embedding); log tau is standardised with training-set mean/std; likelihood is a plain logsumexp, plus a log-survival term 1 − F(T − t_N) for the final censored interval. Reference code: https://github.com/shchur/ifl-tpp. — [arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12127)
- [V] NLL is robust to the number of mixture components across K = 2 to 64 (secondary summary of the paper's ablation). — [Liner review of 1909.12127](https://liner.com/review/intensityfree-learning-of-temporal-point-processes)
- [V] Limitation for marked processes: a later paper argues the original design generates mark and time conditionally independently given history, "removing the connection between mark and time". — [Intensity-free Integral-based Learning of Marked TPPs, arXiv 2308.02360](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.02360)

**Self-Attentive Hawkes, SAHP (Zhang, Lipani, Kirnap, Yilmaz; ICML 2020, arXiv 1907.07561)**
- [R] Self-attention over past events with time-shifted positional encoding; attention output is mapped to three Hawkes-like parameters and the intensity between events is softplus(mu + (eta − mu) exp(−gamma (t − t_j))) — an exponential relaxation from a start level to a base level. Compensator by Monte Carlo/numerical integration. — [Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07561)

**Transformer Hawkes, THP (Zuo, Jiang, Li, Zhao, Zha; ICML 2020, arXiv 2002.09291)**
- [R] Transformer encoder with sinusoidal temporal encoding; intensity lambda_k(t) = softplus(alpha_k (t − t_j)/t_j + w_k·h(t_j) + b_k), i.e. essentially linear-in-time interpolation between events passed through softplus; integral by Monte Carlo or trapezoid; trained with an additional time-prediction (MSE) and type loss. — [Zuo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09291)

**Later models (2020–2026)**
- [R] TriTPP (Shchur, Gao, Bilos, Gunnemann; NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2006.12631): defines the TPP as a triangular map built from spline transforms applied to the whole sequence of compensator values; non-recurrent, so both likelihood and sampling parallelise. — [arXiv 2006.12631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12631)
- [V] Neural flows (Bilos et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2110.13040): replaces neural-ODE solvers with closed-form flows; reports its GRU-flow needs ~60% less time per training epoch than the comparable ODE model, and over an order-of-magnitude speed-up for mixture-based continuous models on real data. — [arXiv 2110.13040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.13040)
- [R] AttNHP (Yang, Mei, Eisner; ICLR 2022, arXiv 2201.00044): attention-based replacement for the CT-LSTM of NHP; intensity at any time t is computed by attending over history from t, so each Monte Carlo integration point costs a full attention pass. — [arXiv 2201.00044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00044)
- [V] Cumulative-hazard line continued: "Cumulative Hazard Function Based Efficient Multivariate TPP Learning" (arXiv 2404.13663) is motivated by fixing FullyNN's invalid CHF. — [arXiv 2404.13663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13663)
- [V] A Nov-2026 NeurIPS 2026 paper (Mehrtens, Buettner, Stegle) reports that a Transformer with a spline intensity head beats earlier "rigid" intensity-based neural models — i.e. the flexible-head-with-exact-integral design keeps winning. — [arXiv 2611.06348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06348)
- [V] 2025 survey covering Bayesian, neural and LLM-based TPPs exists (useful map of post-2022 models). — [Advances in TPPs, arXiv 2501.14291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14291)
- [V] Generative/diffusion-type TPP heads have been explored; one such study clamps sequence length to 1000. — [Exploring Generative Neural TPP, arXiv 2208.01874](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.01874)

### Inferences
- For a single unmarked arrival stream, the attention-vs-RNN question is secondary; the restriction that bites is the between-event functional form. RMTPP (Gompertz), SAHP (single exponential relaxation) and THP (softplus of linear) all impose a per-interval shape no richer than one exponential — i.e. per interval they are not more expressive than the repo's existing sum-of-exponentials Hawkes; their extra power is only in how history sets the parameters.
- A sum-of-exponentials Hawkes is itself a "recurrent" model: with J exponentials the state is J numbers updated in O(1) per event (R_j ← e^{−beta_j dt}(R_j + 1)). A GRU with LogNormMix head strictly generalises "state update + inter-event law", which makes the nested comparison conceptually clean.
- LogNormMix places density on (0, inf) only and is always normalised; unlike a linear Hawkes it cannot be non-stationary/explosive in the supercritical sense when sampled one step ahead, but multi-step simulation can still blow up or die out.
- Mixture heads can place an arbitrarily narrow component at a repeated value (e.g. tau = 0 or tau = 1 ms). This is the mechanism by which tied/discretised timestamps make the NLL diverge (see Section 2/4); the variance floor (clamp on log s_k) is therefore a hyper-parameter that directly controls reported NLL on discretised data.

- No verified source in this session for neural-spline-flow TPP details beyond TriTPP recall; no verified list of 2025–2026 SOTA models other than arXiv 2611.06348 and the 2501.14291 survey.

## 2. Honest benchmarking evidence and evaluation pitfalls

### Takeaway
The independent evidence says (i) there is no consistent winner among neural TPPs, (ii) the decoder/head choice (mixture or other flexible exact head) drives time-NLL more than the encoder, (iii) the "neural beats Hawkes" result largely comes from comparing against the most restricted Hawkes, and disappears when the Hawkes gets flexible baselines and kernels (NeurIPS 2026), and (iv) NLL on continuous densities is fragile: it can be made arbitrarily good on discretised timestamps and is not comparable across normalisations.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Shchur, Turkmen, Januschowski, Gunnemann, "Neural Temporal Point Processes: A Review" (IJCAI 2021, arXiv 2104.03528): many commonly used TPP metrics have subtle failure modes; comparing probabilistic TPPs with point-estimate baselines on MAE/MSE is unfair. — [IJCAI proceedings](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/623); [arXiv 2104.03528](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03528)
- [V] Same review: "the NLL can be 'fooled' on datasets where the arrival times t_i are measured in discrete units, such as seconds" — a flexible model can put narrow density spikes on the discrete values and drive NLL arbitrarily low (attributed to Uria et al. 2013). — [arXiv 2104.03528v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03528v5)
- [V] Same review: "NLL is mostly irrelevant as a measure of error in real-world applications"; a single NLL also hides the split between time and mark error (marks dominate as the number of marks grows). — [arXiv 2104.03528v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03528v5)
- [V] Bosser and Ben Taieb, "On the Predictive Accuracy of Neural TPP Models for Continuous-time Event Data" (TMLR 2023, arXiv 2306.17066): large-scale study over 15 real datasets varying event encoding, history encoder and decoder; "appropriate selection of decoder parameterizations can yield significant gains"; NLL alone gives a limited view, so they add probabilistic calibration; mark distributions of neural TPPs are miscalibrated; several commonly used datasets "may not be suitable" for benchmarking neural TPPs; baselines/datasets/setups differ between papers. Code: https://github.com/tanguybosser/ntpp-tmlr2023. — [arXiv 2306.17066](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17066v2); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3OSISBQPrM)
- [V] EasyTPP (Xue et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2307.08097): open benchmark, five real datasets, implementations of the popular neural TPPs (RMTPP, NHP, FullyNN, SAHP, THP, AttNHP, ODETPP, IntensityFree) plus classical multivariate Hawkes; the ICLR slides state "no consistent winner" on next-event and long-horizon prediction; classical MHP generally trails neural models on next-event prediction. Repo: https://github.com/ant-research/EasyTemporalPointProcess. — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e66233a208ef32f56df6312263239fa0-Abstract-Conference.html); [ICLR slides](https://iclr.cc/media/iclr-2024/Slides/18712_vJKTJVC.pdf)
- [V, secondary source] Per-dataset pattern in EasyTPP: attention models (SAHP/THP/AttNHP) better or comparable on Amazon/Taobao/StackOverflow, NHP top on Retweet and Taxi. — [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/easytpp-towards-open-benchmarking-temporal-point-processes)
- [V] HoTPP benchmark (arXiv 2406.14341): in 6 of 10 long-horizon tasks, simple statistical baselines match or outperform modern marked-TPP models. — [arXiv 2406.14341](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14341v4)
- [V] A 2025 study (arXiv 2507.21616) finds many standard TPP datasets sit in an "information-limited regime" where a simple categorical output head is competitive. — [arXiv 2507.21616](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21616v1)
- [V] Mehrtens, Buettner, Stegle, "Flexible Intensities Matter: A Comprehensive Re-evaluation of Hawkes Processes and Neural TPPs" (arXiv 2611.06348, accepted NeurIPS 2026; 36 pp, 25 tables; TorchPP framework): the reported advantage of neural TPPs comes from comparing flexible neural models with Hawkes processes "in their most restricted form"; once the Hawkes has flexible, learnable baselines and kernels "the previously reported gap largely disappears"; remaining neural gains are mostly in *when* (timing), not *which* (type), with a neural edge on types only on MIMIC-IV. Five standard benchmarks plus UK Biobank and MIMIC-IV tasks. — [arXiv 2611.06348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06348)
- [V] A heartbeat-dynamics TPP paper lists that NLLs are not comparable across sequences of different length or across models and do not test the generative assumptions. — [arXiv 2511.22096](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22096)
- [R] Shchur et al. 2020 report time-NLL as differences relative to LogNormMix rather than absolute values, because inter-event times are normalised and absolute continuous NLLs are scale-dependent. — [arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12127)

### Inferences
- **Unit/rescaling constant.** If times are rescaled t' = c·t, every model's log-density changes by exactly −log c per event (per-event NLL shifts by +log c). Deseasonalised business time is a (piecewise) rescaling, so NLLs in business time and clock time differ by the sum of log-Jacobians. Differences between models evaluated on the *same* transformed data are invariant; absolute numbers are not. Always report the difference versus a homogeneous Poisson (or versus the exponential Hawkes) in nats/event on identical event sets.
- **Per-event vs per-time NLL.** Total log-likelihood over a window [0, T] includes the survival term of the last interval. Dividing by N (per event) or by T (per unit time) are both fine for comparing models on the same window, but they rank windows differently and "per sequence" averages over chunks of different length are not comparable. With chunked neural training the censored last interval of every chunk is often silently dropped while Hawkes code includes the full compensator: make both models score exactly the same N events on the same interval.
- **Chunking/padding and history.** Cutting a multi-million-event stream into fixed windows gives the neural model zero history at the start of each chunk, whereas the Hawkes recursion typically carries state across the whole sample. This biases against the neural model for the first events of each chunk and, more importantly, caps its memory at the chunk length. Fix: carry the GRU hidden state across consecutive chunks (stateful truncated BPTT) or use a burn-in prefix whose loss is masked; apply the identical warm-up convention to the Hawkes model at evaluation.
- **Ties/discretisation.** After aggregation to one event per aggressor order, BTC at tens of millions of events/month still has many same-millisecond pairs. tau = 0 gives log-normal density 0 (NLL = +inf) and tau ≡ k·1 ms lattice lets a mixture spike. Both outcomes are artefacts. See Section 4 for remedies.
- The 2026 re-evaluation implies the repo's right "strong parametric baseline" is not exp or 2-exp with constant baseline, but a multi-exponential (power-law-approximating) kernel with a flexible baseline; a neural TPP is only informative relative to that.

### Gaps
- Exact NLL tables from EasyTPP, Bosser & Ben Taieb (e.g. which decoders rank first, which datasets are flagged unsuitable) and arXiv 2611.06348 could not be read (arxiv blocked). I recall Bosser & Ben Taieb finding LogNormMix-type decoders among the best on time-NLL and the history encoder mattering less; unverified.
- No paper found that benchmarks neural TPPs versus well-specified Hawkes specifically on financial trade arrivals with a unified protocol.

## 3. Finance-specific work

### Takeaway
The finance literature agrees that the trade/price-change self-excitation kernel is slowly decaying (power-law-like over several decades) and that the estimated branching ratio is mostly a function of kernel flexibility, window length and how non-stationarity of the baseline is handled: short-memory kernels on short windows give n ≈ 0.3–0.8, power-law kernels on long windows give n ≈ 1, and controlling for a time-varying baseline rejects criticality. The repo's 0.71 / 0.85 / 0.96 ladder reproduces this known pattern. Neural TPP work in finance is thin, mostly LOB event-type simulation with Neural-Hawkes variants, without rigorous comparison to strong Hawkes baselines.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Bacry, Dayri, Muzy 2012 (EPJ B, arXiv 1112.1838): non-parametric kernel estimation for symmetric multivariate Hawkes from second-order statistics; on high-frequency data finds slowly decaying (power-law) kernels; later work cites it for a power-law kernel over several decades for incoming market orders on Bund and DAX futures. — [arXiv 1112.1838](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.1838); [Bacry, Mastromatteo, Muzy 2015, "Hawkes processes in finance", arXiv 1502.04592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04592)
- [V] Bacry and Muzy (2014; IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 2016 as "First- and second-order statistics characterization of Hawkes processes and non-parametric estimation"): systematic Wiener–Hopf based non-parametric method; the follow-up (Bacry, Jaisson, Muzy, "Estimation of slowly decreasing Hawkes kernels", arXiv 1412.7096) reports faithful estimation of a power-law kernel "over at least 6 decades" and order-book kernels with loose power-law behaviour with exponent close to 1. — [arXiv 1412.7096](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.7096); [Semantic Scholar entry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/First-and-Second-Order-Statistics-Characterization-Bacry-Muzy/1285d19feebbe364f3f09ce24abccd58f551c744)
- [V] Bacry–Mastromatteo–Muzy review: the power-law nature of the kernels was confirmed by Hardiman et al. on E-mini mid-price changes and by Bacry and Muzy on trade arrivals. — [arXiv 1502.04592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04592)
- [V] Hardiman, Bercot, Bouchaud 2013 (EPJ B 86, arXiv 1302.1405): E-mini S&P futures mid-price changes 1998–2011; kernel is power-law with exponent ≈ −1.15 at short times (< ~10^3 s) and ≈ −1.45 over [10^3, 10^6] s; kernel integrates to ≈ 1 independent of period ("markets are and have always been close to criticality"); they attribute Filimonov–Sornette's lower, rising n to an exponential kernel on 30-minute windows that captures only short-term reflexivity. — [arXiv 1302.1405](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.1405)
- [R] Filimonov and Sornette 2012 (Phys. Rev. E 85, arXiv 1201.3572): exponential-kernel Hawkes on short windows of E-mini mid-price changes; n rose from ≈ 0.3 in 1998 to ≈ 0.7–0.8 by 2010. — [arXiv 1201.3572](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.3572)
- [R] Hardiman and Bouchaud 2014 (Phys. Rev. E, arXiv 1403.5227): model-free branching ratio estimator from the mean and variance of event counts in windows, n ≈ 1 − sqrt(mean/variance) for windows long relative to kernel memory — this is the family the repo's "count-variance" estimator (0.96) belongs to. — [arXiv 1403.5227](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.5227)
- [V] Filimonov and Sornette 2015 (Quantitative Finance 15(8), 1293–1314; arXiv 1308.6756): four classes of calibration bias — outliers, the regularisation part of power-law kernels, edge effects, and non-stationarity/intraday seasonality; regime shifts in parameters bias n upward; mixtures of Poisson processes with regime changes produce spurious near-critical n, undercutting Hardiman et al.; likelihood surface is complicated and needs exhaustive search for the global maximum. — [arXiv 1308.6756](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6756); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v15y2015i8p1293-1314.html)
- [V] Same paper on timestamps: many transactions/quote changes have identical timestamps even at nominal ms resolution because of FAST/FIX message packaging; true time is uncertain by at least the inter-packet gap; ignoring this by "assuming millisecond resolution" is called unacceptable; remedy proposed: redistribute timestamps uniformly within the uncertainty interval. — [arXiv 1308.6756](https://arxiv.org/html/1308.6756v3)
- [V] Wheatley, Wehrli, Sornette 2019 (Quantitative Finance 19(7), 1165–1178): near-critical estimates arise from misspecified background dynamics; they use EM estimation with a flexible deterministic baseline, select baseline flexibility by BIC, and "strongly reject" criticality at univariate and bivariate microstructural levels. Follow-up: Wehrli, Wheatley, Sornette 2021 "Scale-, time- and asset-dependence of Hawkes process estimates on high frequency price changes" (Quantitative Finance), controlling inter- and intraday non-stationarity with piecewise-constant immigration chosen by information criteria. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14697688.2018.1550266); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:quantf:v:19:y:2019:i:7:p:1165-1178); [follow-up](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/chfrpseri/rp2039.htm)
- [V] Lallouache and Challet 2016 (Quantitative Finance 16(1), 1–11; arXiv 1406.3967): on FX LOB data at 0.1 s resolution, compares sum-of-exponential kernels and how fit quality depends on calibration-interval length; most Hawkes fits in the financial literature are not statistically significant under formal goodness-of-fit tests, and whole-day significance is effectively unreachable. — [HAL](https://hal.science/hal-01134105); [arXiv 1406.3967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.3967)
- [V] Related: with more flexible kernels, goodness-of-fit tests cannot distinguish forward from time-reversed data, and KS rarely declares kernels significant. — [Testing the causality of Hawkes processes with time reversal, arXiv 1709.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/1709.08516)
- [V] Morariu-Patrichi and Pakkanen, "State-dependent Hawkes processes and their application to limit order book modelling" (Quantitative Finance 22(3), 563–583, 2022): kernels depend on a discrete LOB state that itself is driven by events. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v22y2022i3p563-583.html); [Imperial repository](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/285cb265-364c-4ed2-a715-c597de23728c)
- [V] Shi and Cartlidge, "State Dependent Parallel Neural Hawkes Process for LOB Event Stream Prediction and Simulation" (KDD 2022, pp. 1607–1615, doi 10.1145/3534678.3539462): parallel continuous-time LSTM units, one per event type, plus event–state interaction; reused as a pre-trained "background trader" in a hybrid agent-based LOB simulator (AAMAS 2023 extended abstract, "Neural stochastic agent-based LOB simulation"). — [Bristol repository](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/state-dependent-parallel-neural-hawkes-process-for-limit-order-bo/); [AAMAS 2023 PDF](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~eg/AAMAS2023/pdfs/p2481.pdf)
- [V] Lalor and Swishchuk 2025, "Event-Based LOB Simulation under a Neural Hawkes Process: Application in Market-Making" (arXiv 2502.17417): 12 LOB event types in a Neural-Hawkes framework. — [arXiv 2502.17417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17417v1)
- [V] Kumar (AAMAS 2024 extended abstract): Deep Hawkes variant (LSTM + denoising autoencoder) reported to beat NHP for order type/timing prediction; PAKDD 2024 "Neural Marked Hawkes" conditions marks on an NHP embedding. — [AAMAS 2024](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p2342.pdf); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-97-2259-4_15)
- [V] LOB simulation review (arXiv 2402.17359): Hawkes models are transparent/interpretable versus neural models, but calibration is hard and kernel choice strongly affects predictive power. — [arXiv 2402.17359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17359)
- [V] **Crypto.** Fabre and Muni Toke, "Neural Hawkes: non-parametric estimation in high dimension and causality analysis in cryptocurrency markets" (Quantitative Finance 25(5), 2025; arXiv 2401.09361): despite the name this is NOT an RNN TPP — it solves the second-order-statistics Fredholm (Wiener–Hopf type) equation for the kernel with physics-informed neural networks (moment-based), benchmarks against Wiener–Hopf, estimates volume effect on BTC-USD trade arrival, and causality across 15 crypto pairs on a centralised exchange. Search extracts report a system branching ratio ≈ 0.80 (≈ 78% on a shorter window), a near-critical power-law kernel with exponent close to 1 above roughly 3 ms, and that BTC/ETH are not the most self-exciting (AAVE, DOGE, SOL, LINK larger). — [arXiv 2401.09361](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09361); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14697688.2025.2477673)
- [V] Mark, Sila, Weber, "Quantifying endogeneity of cryptocurrency markets" (European Journal of Finance, 2020/2022): Bitcoin mid-price dynamics show long memory well described by a power-law kernel, at a criticality level similar to fiat FX markets. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1351847X.2020.1791925)
- [V] "Shallow Neural Hawkes" (J. Computational Science 2022): non-parametric Hawkes kernel estimation with a shallow network — again a neural *kernel* inside a linear Hawkes, not a black-box TPP. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1877750322001405)

### Inferences
- The repo's ladder (exp 0.71 < 2-exp 0.85 < count-variance 0.96) is the textbook Filimonov–Sornette vs Hardiman–Bouchaud disagreement in miniature: each added slow timescale captures more of the long tail and raises n, and the count-variance estimator absorbs *any* slow variance (including residual baseline non-stationarity after deseasonalisation, regime shifts, volatility clustering) into n. Per Wheatley et al., the 0.96 should be treated as an upper bound contaminated by exogenous non-stationarity; per Hardiman et al., the 0.71 is a lower bound from kernel truncation.
- The highest-value "ML extension" suggested by this literature is therefore not a black-box TPP but (a) more exponentials on a log-spaced grid (power-law approximation; with rates fixed on a geometric grid the MLE is over weights only and still O(N·J)), plus (b) a flexible (piecewise-constant/spline) baseline selected by BIC as in Wheatley et al. A neural TPP then serves as an *upper-bound benchmark on predictability*: the gap in held-out nats/event between the best Hawkes and the neural model measures how much structure (nonlinearity, inhibition, state dependence) the linear Hawkes family misses.
- Fabre–Muni Toke's crypto n ≈ 0.8 with exponent ≈ 1 beyond a few ms is a directly comparable external datapoint for the repo's 0.71–0.96 range (different exchange/product/period, so indicative only).

### Gaps
- Could not verify numerical details of Bacry–Dayri–Muzy/Bacry–Muzy (exponents, n) from full text; the Emergent Mind claim that Bacry et al. find n "close to unity, > 95% endogenous" is secondary and unverified.
- Magnitudes of each bias class in Filimonov–Sornette 2015 (how much n inflates per mechanism) and their jitter-bias quantification were not retrieved; a search extract mentions a branching-ratio underestimation of about 0.07 in one misspecified-kernel test but the attribution (arXiv 1406.3967 vs 1308.6756) is ambiguous.
- Quantitative results of Shi & Cartlidge (KDD 2022) not retrieved. I found no paper applying an intensity-free/LogNormMix or transformer TPP to Binance (or any crypto) trade arrival times with comparison against multi-exponential Hawkes, and no neural TPP paper on perp futures aggTrades specifically.
- No rigorous published comparison "neural TPP vs power-law Hawkes on trades with time-rescaling residual tests" was found; this appears to be an open niche.

## 4. Model comparison practice (held-out likelihood, residuals, information criteria, timestamp resolution)

### Takeaway
Compare on identical held-out events with identical history warm-up, report nats/event relative to a Poisson reference, and complement NLL with time-rescaling residual diagnostics. Because data are discretised (ms) with ties, either evaluate every model with an interval-censored (discrete) likelihood or evaluate all models on the same uniformly jittered data; never compare a continuous-density NLL on raw lattice timestamps. Expect formal KS tests to reject everything at N ~ 10^6, so use effect-size diagnostics (QQ deviations, residual autocorrelation) rather than p-values.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Time-rescaling theorem as the standard goodness-of-fit tool for point processes (Brown, Barbieri, Ventura, Kass, Frank 2002, Neural Computation): compensator increments of a correctly specified model are i.i.d. Exp(1); assess with KS and QQ plots. — [Brown et al. 2002](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11555987_The_Time-Rescaling_Theorem_and_Its_Application_to_Neural_Spike_Train_Data_Analysis)
- [V] In finance, formal tests on Hawkes residuals almost always reject for long intervals: Lallouache & Challet find whole-day fits at 0.1 s resolution cannot reach significance; calibration-interval length matters. — [HAL](https://hal.science/hal-01134105)
- [V] Flexible kernels make GoF tests weak in a different sense (cannot tell time arrow), so a passed KS is weak evidence of a correct causal model. — [arXiv 1709.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/1709.08516)
- [V] Information criteria for parametric/semiparametric Hawkes: Wheatley et al. choose baseline flexibility with BIC under EM estimation. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14697688.2018.1550266)
- [V] NLL can be driven arbitrarily low on discretely measured timestamps. — [Shchur et al. review, arXiv 2104.03528](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03528v5)
- [V] Timestamp uncertainty and identical stamps in exchange data; uniform redistribution within the uncertainty interval as the pragmatic fix. — [Filimonov & Sornette 2015, arXiv 1308.6756](https://arxiv.org/html/1308.6756v3)
- [V] Randomisation (uniform jitter) itself biases Hawkes estimates, particularly alpha and beta of the exponential kernel (reported in a simulation study in Laub's thesis). — [Laub, "Hawkes Processes: Simulation, Estimation, and Validation"](https://laub.au/pdfs/honours_thesis.pdf)
- [V] Discretised/binned alternatives: Kirchner's INAR(p)-based estimation procedure for Hawkes on binned counts (larger bin width → larger bias); Shlomovich, Cohen, Adams, Patel, "Parameter Estimation of Binned Hawkes Processes" (JCGS 2022), which notes Bernoulli-type approximations that ignore the count per bin "heavily bias results"; FaDIn (Staerman et al., ICML 2023, arXiv 2210.04635) for fast discretised inference with general parametric kernels. — [Kirchner, arXiv 1509.02017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.02017); [Shlomovich et al.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10618600.2022.2050247); [FaDIn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.04635)
- [V] Bosser & Ben Taieb add probabilistic calibration to NLL for TPP evaluation (PIT-style calibration of the predictive inter-event distribution is the neural-TPP analogue of the time-rescaling QQ plot). — [arXiv 2306.17066](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17066v2)
- [R] Uniform dequantisation theory: for discrete data, the expected continuous log-density on uniformly dequantised data lower-bounds the log-probability of the implied discrete model (Theis, van den Oord, Bethge 2016, ICLR, "A note on the evaluation of generative models", arXiv 1511.01844) — the justification for comparing models on jittered timestamps. — [arXiv 1511.01844](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.01844)
- [R] Discrete-time version of time rescaling for binned spike data (Haslinger, Pipa, Brown 2010, Neural Computation 22, "Discrete time rescaling theorem") corrects the KS bias that appears when bin width is not small relative to 1/intensity. — [PubMed Central](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2932849/)
- [R] Shchur, Turkmen, Januschowski, Gasthaus, Gunnemann, "Detecting anomalous event sequences with TPPs" (NeurIPS 2021, arXiv 2106.04465): KS on rescaled inter-event times is insensitive to some misspecifications; proposes a sum-of-squared-spacings statistic as a more powerful GoF test. — [arXiv 2106.04465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04465)

### Inferences (recommended protocol)
1. **Split.** Chronological train / validation / test (e.g. 60/20/20 by days) within each symbol-month; all models scored on the same test events. For the neural model use validation for early stopping; for Hawkes, fit on train (or train+val) and freeze parameters.
2. **Same history.** At the start of the test block, warm both models on the preceding events (Hawkes recursion state; GRU hidden state) without scoring them.
3. **Reference-normalised score.** Report (LL_model − LL_Poisson)/N in nats/event on test, with Poisson rate fitted on train. This cancels the unit/Jacobian constant from business-time rescaling. Bootstrap by day or block to get standard errors on the *difference* between models (paired, per-block).
4. **Discretisation, pick one and apply to all models:**
   - (a) *Jitter*: add U(0, delta) (delta = 1 ms, or 1 µs in later years) in *clock time before* the business-time transform, re-sort, then fit and evaluate everything on the same jittered copy; repeat for a few seeds to show sensitivity. Simple; known to bias exp-kernel alpha/beta when 1/beta approaches delta (Laub). Clamp the mixture scale floor so no component is narrower than the jitter width in log-space.
   - (b) *Interval-censored likelihood*: score P(tau in [tau_obs − delta, tau_obs + delta]) (triangular uncertainty, or [tau, tau + delta]) = F(b) − F(a). LogNormMix has a closed-form CDF so this is free; for sum-of-exponentials Hawkes the between-event survival is closed form too, so F(b) − F(a) = exp(−Lambda(a)) − exp(−Lambda(b)). This gives a proper discrete likelihood, finite for ties (a = 0), comparable across models, and immune to spike-fitting. This is the cleanest option for a unimarked stream.
   - (c) *Aggregate ties*: collapse same-stamp events into one event with a multiplicity mark; model arrivals of "clusters" and multiplicity separately. Changes the object being modelled (n is then for clusters), so only use as a robustness check.
   - (d) *Binned count likelihood* (INAR / binned Hawkes / FaDIn) at delta-width bins: appropriate if bins are fine relative to kernel timescales; costs O(T/delta) rather than O(N), which is heavy at 1 ms over a month (2.6e9 bins) unless sparse.
5. **Residuals.** For each model compute z_i = Lambda(t_{i−1}, t_i): Hawkes via closed-form compensator; LogNormMix via z_i = −log(1 − F(tau_i | h_i)). Then: QQ plot of z vs Exp(1); KS distance (report the statistic D, not just p — at N = 10^6 the 5% critical value is ≈ 1.36/sqrt(N) ≈ 0.0014, so everything rejects); Ljung–Box / autocorrelation of z_i and of u_i = 1 − exp(−z_i) to test independence (the part most diagnostic of missing long memory); sum-of-squared-spacings statistic (Shchur et al. 2021). On discretised data use the jittered or discrete-time-rescaled version, otherwise ties create an atom at z = 0.
6. **AIC/BIC** apply to the parametric fits on the training set (exp: 3 params, 2-exp: 5, J-exp grid: J+1, plus baseline knots). They are not meaningful for a GRU with ~10^4 weights; the common currency across parametric and neural models is held-out LL. With N ~ 10^6–10^7, BIC's penalty (k/2)·log N ≈ 7–8 nats per parameter is negligible against LL differences of even 0.001 nats/event (≈ 10^3–10^4 nats), so in-sample AIC/BIC will essentially always pick the bigger parametric model; out-of-sample LL and parameter stability across months are the more honest discriminators.
7. **Report effect sizes in context**: e.g. gain of 2-exp over exp, J-exp over 2-exp, flexible-baseline over constant-baseline, and neural over best Hawkes, all in nats/event on the same test events; the last number is the "unexplained by linear Hawkes" budget.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published treatment that derives the interval-censored likelihood correction specifically for Hawkes fitted to tied trade timestamps with a branching-ratio bias quantification; the search extract confirms only that jitter biases alpha/beta (Laub thesis) and that Filimonov–Sornette discuss the randomisation procedure. Magnitude of the jitter bias for n at Binance tie rates is unknown and should be measured by simulation in the repo (simulate fitted Hawkes → round to ms → jitter → refit).
- The Theis et al., Haslinger et al. and Shchur et al. 2021 items are recalled, not re-verified this session.

## 5. Extracting a branching ratio or kernel shape from a neural TPP

### Takeaway
There is no established method for reading a branching ratio out of a black-box neural TPP: n is defined for the linear Hawkes cluster representation, and nonlinear neural intensities have no unique kernel. The published "neural + kernel" work in finance keeps the linear Hawkes structure and uses the network only to parameterise or solve for the kernel. For a GRU/LogNormMix model, the defensible comparison is in observable space (simulate from the model, apply the same model-free estimators as on real data) and via averaged impulse responses, not by claiming a neural n.

### Cited Findings
- [V] For linear Hawkes, kernel recovery and Granger-causal graph recovery coincide: process k' does not Granger-cause k iff phi_{k,k'} ≡ 0 (Eichler et al. 2017; Achab et al. 2017; Xu et al. 2016). — [Learning Granger Causality for Hawkes Processes, arXiv 1602.04511](https://arxiv.org/html/1602.04511)
- [V] For neural TPPs the Granger definition "does not carry over automatically" because intensities are nonlinear in history; relations are properly defined only when a linear formulation is retained. Attribution-based workarounds exist: integrated gradients on neural TPPs (Zhang et al. 2020b, CAUSE) and instance-wise self-attentive Hawkes (ISAHP, Wu et al., AISTATS 2024), described as the first neural point process meeting the Granger requirements. — [Shchur et al. review, arXiv 2104.03528](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03528); [Wu et al. 2024, PMLR v238](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/wu24a/wu24a.pdf)
- [V] Neural-kernel-within-linear-Hawkes approaches that do yield a kernel and n: Fabre & Muni Toke (PINN solution of the second-order Fredholm equation; reports branching ratios and kernel diagonals for crypto) and Shallow Neural Hawkes (shallow-network kernel). — [arXiv 2401.09361](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09361); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1877750322001405)
- [V] Non-parametric kernel estimation from second-order statistics (Wiener–Hopf) is model-free with respect to kernel shape and works over ≥ 6 decades for power-law kernels. — [Bacry, Jaisson, Muzy, arXiv 1412.7096](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.7096)
- [V] I found no source on branching-ratio estimation for black-box neural TPPs. — [search summary referencing arXiv 2104.03528 and related](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03528)

### Inferences (how to compare an implied kernel properly)
- **Intensity is available in closed form for LogNormMix**: lambda*(t) = p(t − t_i | h_i) / (1 − F(t − t_i | h_i)). So the fitted hazard as a function of elapsed time, conditional on history state, can be plotted against the Hawkes intensity mu + sum of kernel terms along the same realised history. Compare the two intensity paths directly on held-out data (e.g. log-ratio as a function of time-since-last-event and of recent event count).
- **Averaged impulse response (pseudo-kernel).** For many held-out histories H and probe time s, compute g(u) = E_H[ lambda(s + u | H ∪ {event at s}) − lambda(s + u | H) ] with no further events inserted. For a linear Hawkes this returns phi(u) exactly; for a neural model it is a state-averaged first-order response. Its integral is a "linearised first-generation branching" but is (i) state-dependent — report its distribution across quiet vs busy states rather than one number — and (ii) truncated by the "no further events" survival, because a renewal-type head only defines the law up to the next event. Because the LogNormMix head re-parameterises after every event, the response beyond the next event must be obtained by simulation.
- **Simulation-based comparison in observable space (recommended).** Simulate long paths from the fitted neural model and from each fitted Hawkes; apply to simulated and real data the *same* model-free estimators: count-variance branching estimator (the repo's 0.96 estimator), count autocovariance / Wiener–Hopf non-parametric kernel, inter-event time distribution, Fano factor vs window size. The model whose simulations reproduce the real data's estimator values across scales is the better description; this avoids defining n for the neural model at all. Note that the count-variance estimator applied to a simulated *stationary* neural/Hawkes path is uncontaminated by baseline non-stationarity, which also helps decompose the real-data 0.96.
- **Memory-horizon caveat.** A GRU trained with truncated BPTT over L events and a head conditioned on a 32–64-dim state has effective memory of at most a few hundred to a few thousand events; at BTC rates that is seconds to minutes of business time. A power-law tail extending to 10^3–10^6 s (Hardiman et al.) cannot be represented, so the neural model will look like a *short-memory* process and any implied n will be biased low relative to long-window estimators, for the same reason the exponential kernel gives 0.71. Feeding slow features (EWMA event rates at several horizons — which are exactly sum-of-exponentials Hawkes states) as inputs fixes this cheaply and makes the neural model a strict superset of the multi-exponential Hawkes.
- **Sanity experiment before any real-data claim.** Simulate the repo's fitted 2-exp Hawkes (n = 0.85) at realistic N, round to ms, and train the neural model on it. Expected: held-out LL of neural ≈ true-model LL (gap ≈ 0 within SE); impulse response ≈ phi(u); simulated count-variance n ≈ 0.85. If the neural model cannot recover a known kernel at this N and tie rate, its real-data output is not interpretable.
- If an interpretable kernel is the goal, the better tools are non-parametric but still linear: Wiener–Hopf/second-order estimation (Bacry–Muzy), EM with histogram kernels, or a neural-parameterised kernel inside a linear Hawkes (Fabre–Muni Toke; Shallow Neural Hawkes). The black-box TPP answers a different question: "how much held-out likelihood does the linear Hawkes class leave on the table?"

### Gaps
- No published method or validation for branching-ratio extraction from RNN/transformer TPPs was found; the impulse-response and simulation procedures above are my proposals, not literature results.
- Whether nonlinear effects (inhibition after bursts, saturation) are material for crypto trade arrivals at the aggressor-order level is not established in the sources found.

## 6. Feasibility on CPU (4–16 cores) and best cost-to-insight choice

### Takeaway
No source gives CPU timings for neural TPPs at million-event scale, so feasibility below is engineering inference: a small GRU (32–64 units) with a log-normal-mixture head is the only family whose per-event cost is a single RNN step plus a closed-form logsumexp, with no Monte Carlo integration and no O(L^2) attention, and should train on 1–5 M events in minutes to low tens of minutes per epoch on CPU. Monte Carlo-integrated models (NHP, THP, SAHP, AttNHP) multiply cost by the number of integration samples and are poor value here. This must be confirmed by a 10-minute benchmark in the repo (PyTorch is not currently installed in the research environment, so I could not measure it).

### Cited Findings
- [V] LogNormMix has closed-form likelihood, sampling and moments (no numerical integration). — [arXiv 1909.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12127)
- [R] NHP, THP, SAHP, AttNHP estimate the compensator by Monte Carlo/numerical quadrature at extra time points per event. — [Mei & Eisner 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09328); [Zuo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09291); [Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07561)
- [V] Closed-form-flow models are much cheaper than ODE-based continuous-time models: ~60% less time per epoch for GRU-flow, more than an order of magnitude for mixture-based continuous models. — [Bilos et al. 2021, arXiv 2110.13040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.13040)
- [V] Long sequences are routinely truncated in the neural TPP literature (e.g. clamped to 1000 events), i.e. the standard benchmarks are orders of magnitude shorter than a symbol-month of trades. — [arXiv 2208.01874](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.01874)
- [V] The head matters more than the encoder for timing accuracy (decoder choice yields significant gains), supporting "small GRU + flexible head". — [Bosser & Ben Taieb, arXiv 2306.17066](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17066v2)
- [V] A well-specified flexible Hawkes closes most of the gap to neural TPPs, so the expected neural gain is small and mostly in timing. — [arXiv 2611.06348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06348)
- [V] Available code bases: EasyTPP (PyTorch, includes IntensityFree, NHP, THP, SAHP, AttNHP, RMTPP, FullyNN, ODETPP), ifl-tpp (Shchur), ntpp-tmlr2023 (Bosser). — [EasyTPP](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e66233a208ef32f56df6312263239fa0-Abstract-Conference.html); [ntpp-tmlr2023](https://github.com/tanguybosser/ntpp-tmlr2023)

### Inferences
- **Cost model (unmeasured, order-of-magnitude).** GRU with hidden size H costs ~3·(H^2 + H·d_in) multiply-adds per event forward, roughly 3x that with backward. For H = 64 this is ~4e4 flops/event forward+backward, so even at an effective 1–5 GFLOP/s of realised PyTorch CPU throughput (small-matrix overhead dominates) one should expect on the order of 10^5 events/s, possibly up to ~10^6 with large batches (batch 256–1024 chunks × 128–512 events) and 4–16 threads. That implies ~10–60 s per epoch per million events; 1–5 M-event mid-liquidity symbol-months are comfortably feasible (tens of epochs in under an hour), BTC at tens of millions of events per month is feasible for a few epochs or on a subsample. These numbers are estimates and need a benchmark; the main risk is Python/dispatch overhead per time step when batch is small, so use large batches of chunks and `nn.GRU` over full chunks rather than a Python loop of `GRUCell`.
- **Hawkes cost for reference.** Sum-of-J-exponentials likelihood and gradient are O(N·J) with the standard recursion — sub-second to seconds per evaluation at 10^6 events in vectorised/numba code — so the parametric side is 2–3 orders of magnitude cheaper per fit. Adding exponentials on a fixed log-spaced rate grid (J ≈ 8–15 spanning 1 ms to hours) stays cheap and is the first thing to do.
- **Why not transformers / NHP on CPU.** Attention is O(L^2) per chunk and THP/SAHP/AttNHP/NHP need K extra intensity evaluations per event for the integral (AttNHP: a full attention pass per integration point). With L = 256 and ~10–20 MC points that is a 10–100x cost multiplier over GRU+mixture with no evidence of better timing NLL, and their between-event intensity shapes are rigid. FullyNN is cheap but improper (mass leak) — its NLL would not be comparable with proper models.
- **Windowing/subsampling strategies.**
  - Contiguous chunks of L = 256–1024 events with stateful hidden-state carry-over (detach between chunks) or a masked burn-in prefix (e.g. first 64 events unscored). Never subsample individual events — thinning changes the process (a p-thinned Hawkes is not a Hawkes with the same kernel and has smaller apparent n).
  - Subsample *in blocks*: random contiguous hours/days per epoch; for BTC train on e.g. 2–5 M events drawn as contiguous blocks spread over the month, evaluate on full held-out days.
  - Train per symbol-month, or pool mid-liquidity symbols with a learned per-symbol embedding (the "sequence embedding" option of Shchur et al.) after business-time normalisation so mean inter-event time is ~1.
  - Inputs per event: log tau (standardised), plus cheap slow features — EWMA event counts at several half-lives (these are multi-exponential Hawkes states and extend memory beyond the BPTT window), optionally aggressor side/size if marks are added later.
  - Use `torch.set_num_threads` = physical cores; batch large; float32; early stop on validation nats/event; 3–5 seeds (seed variance of neural NLL must be smaller than the claimed gain).
- **Recommended plan, in order of cost-to-insight.**
  1. (Hours, no ML) Extend the Hawkes family: J-exponential kernel on a log-spaced grid + piecewise-constant/spline baseline with BIC (Wheatley et al.), interval-censored likelihood for ms ties, time-rescaling residual suite. Expect n to move between 0.85 and ~0.96 depending on baseline flexibility — that decomposition is the main scientific result.
  2. (1–2 days) GRU(32–64) + LogNormMix(K = 8–16) with EWMA-rate inputs as a *predictability ceiling*: report held-out nats/event gain over the best Hawkes on 3–5 mid-liquidity symbols and a BTC block subsample. If the gain is < ~0.01 nats/event (to be judged against block-bootstrap SE), conclude "linear multi-timescale Hawkes with flexible baseline is adequate" — consistent with arXiv 2611.06348 — and stop.
  3. (Only if step 2 shows a material gap) Diagnose where it comes from via residuals conditional on state (post-burst inhibition, saturation, side imbalance) and consider a structured extension (nonlinear/state-dependent Hawkes à la Morariu-Patrichi & Pakkanen, or quadratic terms) rather than a bigger network.
  4. Skip: NHP, THP, SAHP, AttNHP, ODE/diffusion TPPs, LLM-based TPPs — high CPU cost, Monte Carlo likelihood noise that complicates sub-0.01-nat comparisons, and no evidence of advantage on unimarked timing.
- **What a neural result can and cannot decide.** It can bound how much held-out likelihood the linear-Hawkes class misses and reveal state dependence. It cannot adjudicate 0.71 vs 0.85 vs 0.96, because that disagreement is about long memory versus baseline non-stationarity (Section 3), which a short-memory GRU does not see.
