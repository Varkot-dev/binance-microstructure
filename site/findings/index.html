Part one · Q0 to Q8

What the market does

Classical market microstructure results, first replicated on BTC and ETH against published equity numbers, then extended across many symbols and five months from June 2023 to July 2026.

Q0Method check

Should one market order count as one trade?

Yes, and getting this wrong changes everything downstream.

Binance prints one market order as many fills. Measuring on raw prints inflates the memory exponent by 0.29 to 0.50, and the inflated value still looks plausible against published ranges. Every later result merges same-millisecond, same-side prints into one aggressor event.

Q1Memory

Do buys follow buys?

Yes, for a very long time. The direction of trades has long memory.

The sign autocorrelation decays as a power law with exponent 0.3803 for BTC and 0.2380 for ETH. A short-lag zigzag in the curve (Q1b) is real market structure, not an artifact of how ties are broken.

Q2 · Q3Price response

How do prices respond to order flow?

They rise and then level off, inside the range theory predicts.

  • The price response grows 5.39× before plateauing, inside the 3.5 to 6.9× band implied by the memory exponent.
  • Price change is linear in order-flow imbalance with R² 0.40, against the 65 to 70% reported for equities.
Q4 · Q4bCross-section

Does memory depend on how busy a market is?

The memory exponent does not. The chance that the next trade flips direction does.

Across 121 symbols, the memory exponent is flat in trading activity (slope −0.0112, R² 0.0003). Flip probability rises with activity (R² 0.2632); tick size is a borderline second factor (t 2.09).

Q5Impact kernel

Why doesn't predictable order flow make prices predictable?

Because each trade's impact fades at the rate that keeps prices close to a random walk.

Separating the bare impact kernel from flow memory, 12 of 16 symbols satisfy the critical-balance condition. This is the mechanism behind the headline of this page: predictable trades, unpredictable prices.

Q6 · Q6bEndogeneity

How much trading is a reaction to other trading?

Most of it: at least 70%, and probably more.

  • Median Hawkes branching ratio 0.7070 from the exponential-kernel fit, a lower bound; 0.959 from the kernel-free count-variance estimator, higher on 41 of 41 symbols.
  • A second kernel timescale raises the fit to 0.854 and closes a median 54% of the gap; the extra component decays in about 7 seconds.
Q7Execution

What does this mean for executing a large order?

Front-loading trades a random cost for a predictable one.

On replayed flow for 6 symbols, front-loading costs more on average (2.11 against 0.55 bps of arrival mid for TWAP), but its standard deviation is 1.75 bps against 29.3, about 17 times lower, and lower on every symbol. This is a cost model, not a backtest.

Q8Over time

Do these results hold from 2023 to 2026?

Partly. The flip law fades, and memory starts depending on liquidity for older contracts.

  • Flip probability against activity: slope +0.1114 in June 2023, +0.0006 by July 2026. On 39 contracts present in both, its t-statistic fell from 4.0 to 1.1.
  • On those same 39 contracts the memory exponent went from no activity dependence (t 1.0) to a clear one (t 3.6); contracts listed since show little.
  • An apparent collapse in endogeneity in 2024 (0.71 to 0.39) is a fitting artifact: the kernel-free estimator moves only from 0.96 to 0.93.
Part two · Q9 to Q14

What can be predicted

Machine learning on top of the classical results, each model scored on held-out days against the baseline that theory already implies, with intervals from resampling whole trading days.

Q9Trade direction

How well can the next trade's direction be predicted?

About 60% · mostly from known memory

Ten symbols, trained on June 2023 and tested on held-out days. Three quarters of the best model's gain over a coin flip is already in the linear predictor built from the memory measured in Q1. Size, timing and recent price moves add most of the rest.

Q10Drift

Does a 2023 model go stale?

Yes, by missing a gain rather than suffering a loss

The 2023 model stays at about 60% in every later year. What changes is the market: a model retrained in 2025 reaches 66%. Staleness here is the accuracy retraining would recover.

Q11Price direction

Can the next price move be predicted, and does it pay?

Predictable · does not pay

Four symbols with top-of-book quotes (2023 only). Direction a few trades ahead is highly predictable where the minimum price step is large relative to the price, but the predicted move is a small fraction of what a round trip costs a taker.

Q12Trade timing

Does a neural network predict trade timing better than Hawkes?

Yes, but Hawkes gets 91% of the way

A recurrent neural point process and Hawkes models with one to three timescales, scored on identical held-out trades with a likelihood that handles millisecond timestamps correctly.

  • The network beats the best Hawkes on 10 of 10 symbols, by a median 0.053 nats per trade.
  • The best Hawkes already captures a median 91% of the network's gain over random arrivals, so Q6's model describes timing well.
  • XRP's Hawkes fits failed (likely a regime change within June), so its larger gap is a poor fit rather than missing structure.
  • This does not change Q6's branching ratios.
Q13Break-even

How far from paying are the price predictions, and are they useful anyway?

Needs fees under 0.65 bps per side · small timing value

Q13 reuses Q11's predictions unchanged and reproduces its numbers exactly, then asks two questions Q11 left open.

At what fee would trading on them pay? The best case for every symbol needs a taker fee under 0.65 bps per side, against the 5 bps assumed. Even a fee of zero would not be enough for 43 of the 144 combinations, because the spread alone is larger than the predicted move.

Can they cut the cost of a trade you must make anyway? A trader who must buy waits when the model predicts the price will dip, and a seller waits when it predicts a rise. That saves 0.22 to 0.36 bps per order on average at the mid price, against roughly zero for waiting at random. The saving is real but small next to its risk: the standard deviation per order is 1.4 to 10 bps, and 31 to 45% of delayed orders end up worse.

Q14Survivorship

Did studying only coins that survived bias the results?

Barely · and the 60% headline is, if anything, low

Q9 and Q10 used 10 large coins that traded in every month studied. Q14 reruns the same prediction test on every coin trading in June 2023 (181, including 45 that later stopped trading), on each dead coin's last full month, and on 113 coins listed after 2023. Stock, ETF and commodity contracts that Binance added in 2026 are labelled and kept out.

  • The biggest factor is how busy a coin is: busier coins are less predictable, by about 4 points of accuracy per tenfold increase in trading.
  • At equal activity, coins that later died were, if anything, slightly more predictable in 2023 (about +1 point for genuine delistings). Renamed coins were no different.
  • New listings in 2026 are no different from the coins that survived from 2023.
  • Q9's 10 coins are among the busiest 5%, so they sit at the 28th percentile for accuracy. The typical coin reaches 61.5%, against Q9's 60.0%. What the models add over a coin flip is typical.

Reading these results

What this does and does not show

Not a trading strategy

No result here claims profit. Q11's cost accounting is optimistic (no latency, no queue, no own impact), and still nothing covers cost.

Few test days

Most test blocks are one or two weeks, so intervals from resampling days are coarse and probably too narrow.

Survivor symbols, checked

The main ML panels use 10 large contracts listed in every month studied. Q14 checks this against 181 contracts, including ones later delisted, and finds little bias.

Quotes only for 2023

Binance publishes top-of-book dumps for 2023 only, so price prediction is limited to that year.

Fee not verified at source

The 5 bps taker fee is the regular tier as reported by third-party pages.

Every model checked against a planted answer

Each estimator and analysis is first tested on synthetic data with a known result; 487 tests pass.
